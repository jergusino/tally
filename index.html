One number, louder with every swipe.

Tally shows the total over the apps you pick. It starts small and white, grows, turns amber, then red, and sends out a ring every 50th swipe. It is semi-transparent, and touches go through to the app underneath.

The default shows swipes with the minutes small under them. You can switch to swipes only, or to time only. Time counts only while one of your apps is in front, and after a short break the count starts again. The exact steps and timing may change before release.

12, 48 and 120 swipes.

The veil closes in. Nothing is blocked.

Edges go dark first. At the end only a small clear patch is left, about a quarter of the screen across. The veil is black, and the number is drawn above it, so the count stays easy to read.

It does not block touches. They pass through to the app underneath, so there is no trap and no button to find. It draws only over the apps you picked, and it never touches system screens or calls.

20, 60 and 150 swipes.

On your home screen too.

Two widgets. The eye widget shows today's swipes and an eye to switch Tally on or off. The time widget shows today's minutes with one plain line about what that time could be.

Tap the eye to pause counting. Tap anywhere else to open Tally. When it is off, the number dims and the label says paused.

These widgets are live. Use the eye, or swipe in the demo. The daily total resets at midnight on your phone's clock, with no history. Widgets are part of the prototype and may change.

Yours to set up.

Tally finds common social apps and turns them on for you. Turn on any other installed app, and turn off any you do not want counted. Browsers are off by default. If you turn one on, the whole browser counts, because Tally does not look at which site you are on.

To count swipes in other apps, Tally uses Android's accessibility service, and it draws the number over them. Android asks you to turn this on yourself. Tally reads no text, messages or posts. It counts scroll events and the time an app is in front.

Home, and Settings. Drawn from the app design, not photos of a phone.

What Tally leaves alone.

  • Your apps. Tally never blocks an app or locks your phone. Touches pass through to the app underneath.
  • Your content. It reads no text, messages or posts from the apps it counts. The counts stay on your phone.
  • Your time. No time limits, no accounts, no streaks, no scores and no history.
  • Your choice. You pick the apps, the style, the veil speed and the hours.
